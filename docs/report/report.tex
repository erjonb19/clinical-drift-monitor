\title{A Drift Monitor for Skin Lesion Classification:\\
Lesion-Level Evaluation, OMOP Research Tables and Real Hospital Shift}
\author{Erjon Brucaj\\\small Personal portfolio project and research prototype. Not for clinical use.}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We describe an open, zero-cost pipeline that trains a skin lesion classifier on four hospital
collections from the ISIC Archive, scores every image with an out-of-distribution (drift)
detector, and evaluates both on two hospitals held out entirely. The data land in a Databricks
medallion lakehouse with data-quality gates that fail the update, and are also published as
OMOP CDM v5.4-style research tables with every source value mapped to a cited concept. Every
test result comes from a specification committed before a single look at the test split, and
every reported number is logged as an MLflow run tied to a committed file.
The shipped ensemble reaches 66.46\% balanced accuracy on the training hospitals' test split
and falls to 55.91\% and 39.40\% at the two new hospitals, where the shipped Mahalanobis
detector separates the new images with AUROC 67.8\% and 82.3\%. A frozen BiomedCLIP baseline
trails the ensemble by 15 to 16 points, and subgroup analysis shows uneven drift-alert rates by
site.
We report the limits we measured: splits are by lesion rather than patient at one site, detector
scores do not reproduce between GPU and CPU, darker skin types are too rare to judge, and site
is confounded with imaging type.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A classifier that performs well on its test split can fail quietly when the images change:
a new hospital, a new camera, a new patient population. A drift monitor scores each incoming
image for how unlike the training data it is, so that a shift can be flagged before labels
arrive. This report covers the data engineering and evaluation behind such a monitor for
dermoscopic and clinical skin lesion images, with an emphasis on evaluation that cannot be
quietly tuned to the test set, and on research data that others can reuse.

The work grew out of a graduate course project whose original notebook split images rather
than lesions, so images of one lesion appeared on both sides of the split. Fixing that, and
measuring what it changes, is where the project began.

\section{Data and lakehouse}
\paragraph{Sources.} 27{,}656 images from the ISIC Archive. Training clients: HAM10000
\cite{tschandl2018} (Vienna and Queensland), Barcelona and MSK. Held out as new hospitals: Buenos
Aires and PAD-UFES-20 \cite{pacheco2020}, the latter all smartphone photographs. Two benchmark sets (PathMNIST
histopathology and CIFAR-10 natural images) only check that a detector works at all.

\paragraph{Medallion pipeline.} Images and metadata are landed with Auto Loader into bronze,
decoded and joined into silver, and summarised into gold, as Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines on
Databricks Free Edition. Fifteen data-quality gates fail the update rather than warn; a
companion job feeds a deliberately broken subset through the same pipeline and must fail at
the gate. Splits are by lesion and frozen across data versions.

\paragraph{OMOP research tables.} The same silver tables are published as OMOP CDM
v5.4-style person, observation\_period, condition\_occurrence, procedure\_occurrence,
image\_occurrence (following the OHDSI medical imaging extension proposal), cdm\_source and
concept tables: 15{,}196 persons, 16{,}238 conditions, 37{,}514 procedures and 27{,}656
images. Every diagnosis, sex, body site, confirmation method and modality value maps to an
OMOP concept looked up in an Athena download, or to concept 0 with a stated reason; seven
quality gates check keys, foreign keys, mapping completeness, concept resolution, row counts,
one label per lesion and required columns. Unity Catalog lineage runs from bronze into every
OMOP table. A person is a real patient only where ISIC records a patient identifier
(2{,}776 people); elsewhere one person is one lesion, an assumption that over-counts people.
Dates are placeholders because the sources record none.

\section{Models and detectors}
\paragraph{Classifier.} EfficientNet-B0, ImageNet-pretrained and fine-tuned at 224 pixels with
class-weighted cross-entropy, trained on Databricks serverless GPU; architecture, image size and
training length were chosen on validation, and three seeds are averaged into the shipped
ensemble. Flower FedAvg and FedProx versions treat the four training hospitals as clients.

\paragraph{Drift detectors.} Four scores are computed the same way for in- and
out-of-distribution images, with statistics fit on training data only: Mahalanobis distance to
the nearest class mean in a PCA space sized to keep 95\% of training-feature variance
\cite{lee2018}; Gram-matrix deviations \cite{sastry2020}; the negative maximum softmax
probability \cite{hendrycks2017}; and the free energy \cite{liu2020}. Each is reported as AUROC
and as the false-positive rate at 95\% true-positive rate.

\paragraph{Baseline and explanations.} BiomedCLIP \cite{zhang2023} is used frozen, zero-shot and
with a three-seed linear probe; Grad-CAM \cite{selvaraju2017} maps are checked against a
re-initialised network \cite{adebayo2018}.

\section{Evaluation design}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Splits by lesion,} never by image, enforced by a test.
\item \textbf{One look at test.} Every test result follows a specification committed to the
repository before the look, and a lock file records the look. Choices (architecture, prompt
templates, probe hyperparameters, detector settings, thresholds) are made on validation only.
\item \textbf{Seeds and intervals.} Three seeds, reported as mean and range; 95\% bootstrap
intervals with 1{,}000 resamples by patient where ISIC records one, else by lesion.
\item \textbf{Same scoring for in- and out-of-distribution data,} with detector statistics fit
on training data only, and a test that scores point the right way.
\item \textbf{Evidence.} Each results file is logged as an MLflow run on Databricks, and an
index maps file hashes to runs; CI fails if a logged file changes. A list of past silent
failures (bugs that reported success while being wrong) records the check that now catches
each one.
\item \textbf{Real and staged drift are never mixed.} Every shift in this report is a real,
separate dataset.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}
\paragraph{Classification.} On the pooled client test split (3{,}262 images, 1{,}857 lesions),
the shipped ensemble of three EfficientNet-B0 seeds reaches 66.46\% balanced accuracy
[60.24, 72.85], 71.62\% melanoma sensitivity [64.21, 78.14] and 90.90\% macro AUROC; a single
model averages 62.99\% (range 62.21--64.28). Federated training with Flower (FedAvg 57.14\%,
FedProx 58.98\%) trails centralized training, and its rounds were selected on pooled validation,
which a real federation could not see.

\paragraph{Real hospital shift.} At the held-out hospitals the ensemble falls to 55.91\%
[52.3, 59.6] at Buenos Aires and 39.40\% [36.3, 42.3] at PAD-UFES-20, with melanoma sensitivity
46.64\% and 25.00\%. A melanoma threshold set on validation for 95\% sensitivity holds on the
client test split (96.2\%) and drops to 81.8\% and 78.8\% at the new sites: the silent
degradation a drift monitor exists to catch.

\paragraph{A vision-language baseline.} A frozen BiomedCLIP model, with prompt templates and a
linear probe chosen on validation and one test look, reaches 30.09\% balanced accuracy zero-shot
and 50.84\% with the probe (seed range 50.33--51.24). Paired on the same resamples, each probe
seed is 15.2 to 16.1 points below the ensemble, every interval excluding zero. One exception: on
PAD-UFES-20, zero-shot melanoma sensitivity exceeds the ensemble's (71.15\% against 25.00\%).
On BiomedCLIP features, Mahalanobis separates the real sites well (AUROC 0.752 and 0.959), but
maximum softmax probability scores below chance on every shift: the out-of-distribution images
receive a \emph{higher} top probability than the test images. We checked the score's direction
before reporting this.

\paragraph{Subgroups and bias.} Under a committed specification and two reproduction gates, the
ensemble is weakest at Barcelona among the training hospitals (53.6\%, against 67.9--69.5\%
elsewhere) and in patients under 30 (27.2\%, a group of mostly nevi in which the rarer classes
are missed). Within each held-out site, intervals for Fitzpatrick types I to III overlap, and
types IV to VI (98 images in total) cannot be judged. A drift alert set to fire on 5\% of client
test images fires unevenly by site, most of all on BiomedCLIP features (13.3\% of MSK images
against 1.4\% of HAM10000 Queensland), which makes the alert rate itself a fairness question for
a monitor.

\paragraph{Interpretability for review.} Grad-CAM maps from the shipped checkpoint for 150
images (the worst misses per class and the most out-of-distribution) feed a worksheet for a
human reviewer. Against a re-initialised network the mean rank correlation is 0.045, so most maps
depend on the learned weights; 11 maps exceed 0.5 and are flagged as not interpretable. Maps agree
across seeds at 0.81 on average. The commonest error is a nevus called melanoma (199 of 3{,}262
test images). No map has yet been reviewed by a clinician.

\section{Governance and engineering}
The repository keeps a list of \emph{silent failures}: eleven bugs that reported success while
being wrong, each with the check that now catches it. Among them: the course notebook reported a
Mahalanobis AUROC of 4.1\% because it scored in-distribution images its PCA had been fit on, used
each image's true class for in-distribution but the nearest class for out-of-distribution, and
projected one benchmark with a PCA refit on itself; a lesion-level split passed every gate while
patients spanned train and test; and a cohort service's suppressed counts could be worked out by
subtraction. Long-running jobs are pinned to a commit: the wheel and every input file are
hash-checked inside the job, and a test look writes its lock only after its results are saved,
so a crash cannot use up the look. The OMOP tables feed a governed cohort service whose
definitions run only after a reviewer approves their exact hash, with small counts suppressed
and every decision in a hash-chained audit log.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Splits are by lesion, not patient.} At MSK, 47 patients have lesions in two
splits; HAM10000 and Barcelona record no patient identifier. Dropping the affected test images
moves the ensemble's balanced accuracy from 66.46\% to 66.09\%.
\item \textbf{Detector scores do not reproduce between GPU and CPU.} A CPU refit of the Gram
detector missed the GPU fit's normaliser by up to 11.8\%, so monitoring baselines must be built
on the serving hardware.
\item \textbf{Darker skin cannot be judged.} Skin type is recorded only at the two held-out
sites, with 98 images of Fitzpatrick types IV to VI in total.
\item \textbf{Site and imaging type are confounded.} PAD-UFES-20 is all smartphone photographs,
so part of its drop may come from imaging type rather than the hospital.
\item \textbf{Public research images, not patient records.} The OMOP tables describe image
collections; their persons, dates and visits are approximations stated column by column.
\end{enumerate}
