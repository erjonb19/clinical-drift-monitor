# Model card: skin lesion classifier with a drift score (v2)

> **Model card.** Structured after, not compliant with, the CHAI Applied Model Card (mc-schema v0.2). This model is a research prototype and **is not for clinical use**: it must not be used to diagnose, screen or triage patients. Numbers are generated by `scripts/build_docs.py` from committed evidence files (named beside each number) and are not edited by hand.

Sections follow the order of CHAI's Applied Model Card v0.2 schema
(github.com/coalition-for-health-ai/mc-schema, `v0.2/schema.xsd`). A crosswalk to the nine
categories of HTI-1 predictive decision support intervention source attributes and to HTI-1's
risk areas is at the end.

## 1. Basic info
- **Model name:** Clinical Imaging Drift Monitor skin lesion classifier, v2: a 3-seed ensemble of
  EfficientNet-B0 models, with a Mahalanobis out-of-distribution (drift) score.
- **Model developer:** Erjon Brucaj (personal portfolio project, solo work).
- **Developer contact:** issues on the project's GitHub repository (erjonb19/clinical-drift-monitor).

## 2. Release info
- **Release stage:** research prototype; not released. The API and its Docker image are built
  and tested; a free-tier public demo is prepared but not deployed (on hold pending the owner's decision on public weights).
- **Release date / version:** not released; evaluated models are the v2 checkpoints recorded in
  `results/phase2/v2/training/final_models.json`.
- **Global availability:** none. **EHR compatibility:** none. **Regulatory approval:** none.

## 3. Model summary
Classifies a dermoscopic image of a skin lesion into seven HAM10000 classes (akiec, bcc, bkl,
df, mel, nv, vasc) and returns a Mahalanobis distance that flags images unlike the training
data. Its purpose in this project is to demonstrate drift monitoring: detecting when the model
sees data it was not built for. **Keywords:** dermoscopy, skin lesion classification,
out-of-distribution detection, drift monitoring, federated learning, research prototype.

## 4. Uses and directions
- **Intended use and workflow:** a research and engineering demonstration of monitoring a
  medical-imaging classifier for site and acquisition drift. No clinical workflow.
- **Primary intended users:** ML and MLOps engineers, and reviewers of this portfolio project.
- **How to use:** offline evaluation from the repository's notebooks and scripts, or the API in
  `app/api` (Docker), which returns probabilities, the drift score and an abstain flag.
- **Targeted patient population:** none clinically. The data are dermoscopic images from the
  four client sources (HAM10000 Vienna and Queensland, Barcelona, MSK).
- **Cautioned out-of-scope settings:** any diagnosis, screening, triage or referral of real
  patients; clinical (non-dermoscopic) photographs, including smartphone photos; primary care or
  other low-prevalence settings; darker skin types (Fitzpatrick IV to VI are barely represented;
  skin type is recorded only at the two held-out sites); lesion classes outside the seven.

## 5. Warnings
**Known risks and limitations**
- **Sensitivity falls at new sites.** At a threshold set on validation for 95% melanoma
  sensitivity, the ensemble's sensitivity is 96.2% on the clients' test split but
  81.8% at Buenos Aires and 78.8% at PAD-UFES-20 (`results/phase2/triage/test.json`).
- **Imaging type is confounded with site.** All client images are dermoscopic; PAD-UFES-20 is
  entirely smartphone clinical close-ups and Buenos Aires is partly clinical images.
- **Label provenance.** Every melanoma label at every site is confirmed by histopathology, so
  sensitivity rests on biopsy-proven labels. Specificity rests partly on benign labels confirmed
  without biopsy (serial imaging, single-image expert consensus, confocal microscopy), especially
  outside MSK. Histopathology share of all labels: MSK 97.7%, Barcelona 75.2%, Buenos Aires 64.1%, PAD-UFES-20 58.4%, HAM10000 53.3%
  (`results/phase2/label_provenance/label_provenance.json`). 578 Buenos Aires images have no
  confirmation method recorded (a provenance gap).
- **Bordered images.** 19.5% of training images have dark (vignetted) corners, mostly
  from Barcelona; Buenos Aires is 31.8% bordered (`results/phase2/drift/borders.json`).
- **Validation-to-test gap.** The configuration was chosen on validation across many compared
  variants. On the full v2 sets, balanced accuracy is 2.4 to 3.1 points lower on test than on validation for the centralized single models and 1.8 points lower for the ensemble; for the six federated models it is mixed (2 of 6 score higher on test).
- **Drift scores depend on hardware.** Mahalanobis scores recomputed on CPU differed from the GPU
  run's by up to about 5% (rank order nearly identical); Gram's normalizer by up to 11.8%. A
  baseline and the batches compared with it must be scored on the same setup
  (`results/phase2/batch_drift/reference-186825054715071/`).
- **Federated results are optimistic.** Federated rounds were selected on pooled validation, which
  a real federated deployment could not see.

**Known biases or ethical considerations:** the four training sources are European, Australian
and US dermatology collections. Skin type is recorded only at the two held-out sites; within
them, types I to III are assessed (section 7) and types IV to VI are too rare to judge. Barcelona is 39.3% of training images and therefore also of FedAvg's
aggregation weight.

**Clinical risk level:** not assessed. The model is not for clinical use.

## 6. Trust ingredients
### AI system facts
- **Outcomes and outputs:** seven class probabilities; a Mahalanobis drift score (PCA size by the
  method-D rule, 95% of training-feature variance); for the triage analysis only, a referral
  flag when the melanoma probability is at or above 0.0638.
- **Model type:** convolutional neural network (EfficientNet-B0, 224 px, centre view), three seeds
  averaged; class-weighted cross-entropy.
- **Foundation models:** torchvision's ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B0, fine-tuned.
- **Input data source:** ISIC Archive public collections (Barcelona/BCN20000, MSK, Buenos Aires,
  PAD-UFES-20) and HAM10000, each image's licence recorded (`docs/DATA_CARD.md`).
- **Output and input data types:** input: one RGB image (JPEG); output: probabilities and a score.
- **Development data characterization:** 15,277 training, 3,210 validation and
  3,262 test images, split by lesion; training shares HAM10000 Vienna 35.7%, HAM10000 Queensland 10.4%, Barcelona 39.3%, MSK 14.6%
  (`results/phase2/v2/training/data_summary.json`).
- **Bias mitigation approaches:** lesion-level splits (no lesion in two splits, gated); class-
  weighted loss; per-site, bordered/non-bordered, skin-type, age, sex and body-site reporting
  (research Phase C). No mitigation beyond reporting.
- **Serving and abstention:** a FastAPI service on CPU (`app/api`) returns the seven probabilities, the drift score and an abstain flag when the score is above 847.96, the 95th percentile of validation scores. Share of images flagged for review: validation 5.0%; clients' test split 6.6%, Buenos Aires 17.9%, PAD-UFES-20 30.4%. Keeping only the most confident 80% of Buenos Aires images raises balanced accuracy from 55.9% to 61.0% (`results/phase3/serving/results.json`, risk-coverage curves for confidence and drift score). The Docker image is built and smoke-tested in CI with an untrained test bundle; the real bundle was checked once in Docker locally; it is not deployed.
- **Ongoing maintenance:** a monthly Lakeflow job replays 12 months of real images (months 1 to 4 from the clients' test split, then Buenos Aires mixed in, then PAD-UFES-20; `results/phase3/monthly/spec.json`). Alerts (KS p < 0.01 and PSI at least 0.2) in months 9, 10, 11, 12 (PSI 0.62 to 4.01); warnings in months 7, 8; no alert in months 1 to 4. Per-skin-type alerts (type II, the only type with 100 images in a month) in months 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12: skin type is recorded only at the new sites, so these alerts measure the site shift as much as skin type (in months 7 and 8 the type II group, all Buenos Aires images, alerted while the whole month only warned). Every scored image is written to the `gold_prediction_audit` table with the bundle's manifest hash and the input's hash. Retraining: one retraining run under `results/phase3/retrain/spec.json`, triggered by months 7 to 9 (the drift table's alert flag also counts the type II alerts of months 7 and 8). Fine-tuned on the clients' training split plus 653 labelled Buenos Aires images; on 301 images of Buenos Aires patients the challenger never saw, balanced accuracy 56.7% (champion) and 62.9% (challenger), a gain of +6.2 points with a paired lower bound of +2.0; client validation change -1.6 points (limit -1.0). Decision: not promoted; the champion was kept (`results/phase3/retrain/decision.json`). The registered model
  `workspace.cdm.skin_lesion_classifier` v1 (alias champion) reloads through
  the registry and matches the bundle's answers on 3 made-up images (`results/phase3/lineage/registered_model_check.json`).
  A CI test holds the shipped model's committed metrics above floors (`config/eval_gate.json`).
- **Security and privacy of the API:** uploads are read as a capped stream (10 MB) and never
  stored or logged; the access log is off and only counts and timing are logged; requests are
  rate-limited; the container runs as a non-root user. The Docker CI job checks that no image
  reaches the disk or the log.
- **Transparency:** code, configuration, evidence files and the silent-failure log are public in
  the repository.
- **Explanations:** Grad-CAM maps for 150 client-test images (the worst misses per class and the most out-of-distribution), from the shipped seed-0 checkpoint, are on a review worksheet (MLflow artifact and `workspace.cdm.gold_review_worksheet`). Weight-randomization check: mean rank correlation 0.045 (range -0.63 to 0.64) with a re-initialized network; 11 maps are flagged as not interpretable. Mean agreement across seeds 0.81. The maps are coarse (7x7) and **not reviewed** by a clinician (`results/research/d_review/results.json`).

### Transparency information
- **Funding source:** none (zero-cost project). **Third-party information:** dataset providers as
  listed in `docs/DATA_CARD.md`. **Stakeholders consulted:** none (no clinicians or patients were
  consulted; a stated gap).

## 7. Key metrics
### Usefulness, usability and efficacy
- **Goal:** classify dermoscopic images into seven classes on held-out lesions.
- **Results (internal, clients' test split, 3,262 images, 1,857 lesions;
  `results/phase2/v2/test/final_test.json`, 95% bootstrap intervals by lesion):**

| Model | Balanced accuracy | Macro AUROC | Melanoma sensitivity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3-seed ensemble (shipped) | 66.5% [60.2%, 72.9%] | 90.9% [87.3%, 94.6%] | 71.6% [64.2%, 78.1%] |
| Single model, mean of 3 seeds | 63.0% (range 62.2%–64.3%) | 89.7% (range 89.0%–90.4%) | 68.5% (range 66.4%–70.1%) |

- **Interpretation:** the ensemble is better than any single model; intervals are wide (about ±6
  points), partly because some classes have few test images.
- **Test type:** internal validation on a lesion-level held-out test split, one look per model.
- **Validation process:** configuration chosen on validation only; v2 kept over v1 by a rule set
  in advance (pooled validation balanced accuracy +1.05 points, needed +1.00;
  `results/phase2/v2/decision.json`).

### Safety and reliability
- **Goal:** know when the model is outside its data, and how its errors move at new sites.
- **Results:** drift detection, centralized models, mean over 3 seeds (`results/phase2/drift/v2/table.json`):

| Detector | Buenos Aires | PAD-UFES-20 | PathMNIST | CIFAR-10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mahalanobis (shipped) | 67.8% AUROC, 85.7% FPR@95 | 82.3% AUROC, 78.3% FPR@95 | 94.5% AUROC, 23.5% FPR@95 | 96.4% AUROC, 20.7% FPR@95 |
| Gram | 72.6% AUROC, 73.3% FPR@95 | 77.2% AUROC, 76.9% FPR@95 | 98.5% AUROC, 6.6% FPR@95 | 97.6% AUROC, 13.0% FPR@95 |
| Energy | 60.3% AUROC, 88.7% FPR@95 | 77.2% AUROC, 79.0% FPR@95 | 87.8% AUROC, 46.9% FPR@95 | 92.3% AUROC, 38.2% FPR@95 |
| Max softmax | 54.4% AUROC, 91.8% FPR@95 | 66.8% AUROC, 87.4% FPR@95 | 79.6% AUROC, 67.5% FPR@95 | 81.9% AUROC, 62.8% FPR@95 |

  The shipped detector was chosen by a rule fixed in advance (highest mean AUROC on the two real
  sites): Mahalanobis 75.05% against Gram 74.87%, a near tie within the
  seed spread; Gram has the lower false-alarm rate.

  Triage operating point (triage framing, not a clinical claim; threshold set on validation for
  95% sensitivity; `results/phase2/triage/test.json`). Specificity is the metric that carries
  between settings; the referral rate depends on prevalence (about 18% here) and does not:

| Group | Images | Melanomas | Specificity | Melanoma sensitivity | Referral rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| Client test, pooled | 3,262 | 599 | 52.3% [49.7%, 55.2%] | 96.2% [93.6%, 98.2%] | 56.6% |
| Buenos Aires (external) | 1,458 | 253 | 59.1% [56.4%, 62.1%] | 81.8% [76.7%, 86.3%] | 48.0% |
| PAD-UFES-20 (external) | 2,106 | 52 | 62.5% [60.2%, 64.7%] | 78.8% [65.5%, 89.5%] | 38.6% |

  Calibration: the ensemble's expected calibration error on test is 2.00%; temperature
  scaling fitted on validation made it worse (3.56%), so the ensemble is used uncalibrated.
- **External validation:** Buenos Aires and PAD-UFES-20 never contributed to training or model
  choice. Seven-class results, labelled images only, with the analysis look's class mapping

own committed specification and one test look (research use only, never served):

| Model | Balanced accuracy | Melanoma sensitivity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BiomedCLIP zero-shot | 30.1% [24.9%, 34.7%] | 42.7% |
